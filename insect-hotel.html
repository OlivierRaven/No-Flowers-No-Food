Build an Insect Hotel

Simple, proven steps to support native pollinators and beneficial insects in Aotearoa. Use natural, dry materials, get the hole sizes right, place it in full morning sun, and keep it clean each year.

How to build Placement Maintenance
Why it helps

Give pollinators safe nesting spaces

Most native solitary bees and many beneficial insects need small, dry cavities to nest. An insect hotel provides those cavities using untreated wood, bamboo, and paper tubes. Successful hotels are compact, sunny, rain-sheltered, and maintained once a year.

Step-by-step

How to build an effective insect hotel

1) Choose the frame

Use a small wooden box or a simple frame (e.g., 25–35 cm wide × 25–35 cm high × 10–15 cm deep). Add a solid back panel to keep wind and rain out. Avoid plastics and treated timber.

  • Untreated, seasoned timber
  • Weatherproof roof with a little overhang
  • Mounting points (two screws through back)

2) Fill with nesting materials

Mix cavity types to suit different species. Ensure all cavities are clean-cut, smooth inside, and closed at the back.

  • Drilled hardwood blocks: 3–10 mm diameter holes, 10–15 cm deep, closed at the back.
  • Bamboo/pūkekohekohe canes: cut between nodes so one end is closed; internal diameters 3–10 mm.
  • Paper/cardboard tubes: thick-walled, snug in the frame; avoid flimsy craft paper.

3) Hole sizes that work

Provide a range of diameters for solitary bees and wasps.

  • 3–4 mm (small species)
  • 5–7 mm (common sizes)
  • 8–10 mm (larger species)

Space holes at least 8–10 mm apart to keep the block strong.

4) Prepare the surfaces

Drill into the face grain of hardwood (not end grain) to avoid splitting and rough interiors. Lightly sand entrances to remove splinters.

5) Keep it dry

Add a sloped roof and a small overhang. If the frame is exposed, fit a simple rain cap or mount under an eave.

6) Secure the fillings

Pack tubes tightly so they don’t rattle. Use a simple front lattice or wire mesh only if needed; don’t obstruct entrances.

Site it right

Placement and orientation

Sun

Full morning sun is best (faces north-east to north in NZ). Warmth helps larvae develop and discourages mould.

Height

Mount 0.8–1.5 m above ground on a stable fence, wall, or post. Keep the back solid and the front clear of vegetation.

Proximity to flowers

Place within 5–20 m of pesticide-free flowers and shrubs. Add a shallow water dish with pebbles nearby.

Visibility matters. Add a small sign so people know it’s an intentional pollinator habitat.
Avoid these

Common mistakes that make hotels fail

Don’t use

  • Treated/preserved wood or plastics
  • Loose, short, or cracked tubes
  • End-grain drilled logs (splinters, water ingress)
  • Straw/pinecones alone (fine as filler, poor for nesting)

Don’t place

  • In deep shade or damp spots
  • Under roof drip lines with heavy splashback
  • Where lawn sprinklers hit directly
  • On moving branches or wobbly posts
Care

Maintenance and yearly cycle

Inspect quarterly

Check for mould, bird damage, or loose tubes. Replace any soggy or splintered materials.

Annual refresh

After the main emergence (late spring/early summer), retire heavily used paper tubes and insert new ones. Timber blocks last longer but still inspect holes and entrances.

No pesticides

Keep the surrounding area spray-free. Herbicides and insecticides undermine the hotel’s purpose.

Tip: Build two small hotels and rotate materials each year — one “active”, one “resting/cleaned”.

Aotearoa context

Designed for local species

In Aotearoa, many solitary bees (e.g., masked and leafcutter bees) use small, dry cavities. Mixed diameters (3–10 mm) and clean, deep holes support a range of species. Pair your hotel with year-round flowers, bare soil patches for ground-nesters, and no-mow strips to supply forage.

Checklist

Quick build checklist

Materials

  • Untreated, seasoned timber frame with solid back
  • Hardwood blocks drilled 3–10 mm × 10–15 cm deep
  • Bamboo canes with one end naturally closed
  • Thick-walled paper/card tubes (closed at back)
  • Fixings to mount securely; sloped rain cap

Setup

  • Full morning sun (NE–N facing)
  • 0.8–1.5 m high on stable surface
  • Near pesticide-free flowers and shrubs
  • Tubes snug; entrances smooth and unobstructed
  • Simple sign explaining the purpose
